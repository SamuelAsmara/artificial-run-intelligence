intervals.icu: connect with one field.

The athlete used to hunt for two values on the intervals.icu settings page
— the athlete id and the API key — and paste both. intervals.icu accepts
`0` as "the athlete this key belongs to", so the key alone identifies the
account. The form is now one field: the settings page opens in a small
window beside Runi instead of a tab that hides it, three steps say what to
do there, the athlete pastes the key, and the server asks intervals.icu
whose it is. The reply greets them by name with what was found —
"Connected as Sami Asmara · 130 runs and 90 nights found". A pasted blob
that also contains the id is split apart quietly; an id passed explicitly
is still accepted and verified.

Checked against the real intervals.icu settings page: Developer Settings
has no anchor to deep-link to, and the key sits behind "(view)" next to a
pencil that regenerates it. So the steps name the right control, and the
form shows a small sketch of the block to look for. The settings window
opens at 980px so their two-column layout holds and the section stays
where the steps say it is. 809 tests pass.

Your numbers: a board, not a page of formulas — and a tab of its own.

/settings/methodology was every formula in Runi as prose and equations,
correct and unread. It is now /numbers, a top-level tab beside Home, Plan
and Activities: thirteen tiles, each carrying the athlete's own current
value and a status band, laid out as the pipeline they actually form —
what comes in (heart rate, pace, weekly volume, recovery) → load →
fitness & fatigue → form & load ratio → readiness, with the per-run
figures (GAP, cardiac drift, Riegel) in a row beneath. Wires between the
lanes meet the tiles' centres; readiness is lit.

Click a tile and one panel under the board explains it in four short
blocks: the letters, your numbers (the arithmetic with the athlete's own
values substituted — "47 − 39 = +8"), the formula in one line, and why
that formula, with the source in a word. Readiness is open on arrival.
The selected tile lives in the URL hash (/numbers#tsb) so a coach can
link an athlete to one figure. On phones the lanes stack and the panel
opens under the tapped tile.

The values come from the same pipeline the readiness job runs
(buildSnapshots for thresholds, sessionLoad for the last run's load), so
a number here is the number the dashboard would show. Bands mirror the
product's own: form zones from readinessSubscores, zones from LTHR, the
1.5 load-ratio threshold the safety checker acts on. The race prediction
is Riegel from the longest personal best on file — the product's first
real prediction; the dashboard rail used to say "No prediction yet".

Every athlete screen's top nav gained Numbers; the settings cards point
there; the old address redirects.

Each panel opens with the figure's scale drawn as a bar: the bands the
product uses, tinted by what they mean (rest / go easy / ready; loaded /
neutral / fresh / detraining; the six heart-rate zones as % of LTHR; the
7–9% volume ramp and the step-back week), with the athlete's value
standing on it. Two of the bars carry two markers, because the point is
the relation: fitness and fatigue on one axis (the gap is form), and the
Riegel prediction against the target finish time. Every band edge is the
same threshold the code acts on, so the picture and the behaviour agree.

Three ways to a plan, and a plan of one's own

/plan with no plan now offers the three routes side by side: join a coach
(the code, right there), take Runi's plan, or write your own. Runi's plan
is previewed before it exists — length, the four phases as an arc, one

Coach: cycles are now something the coach runs

A cycle is a named group preparing for one race day from one template —
"Marathon prep · Tel Aviv" — created on the Cycles screen (or from a
template's "Start a cycle" button, which arrives with the distance set)
and filled by adding athletes from the roster. Adding someone writes the
cycle's race as their goal race and builds their plan from the cycle's
template starting that week, so the screen can show each member's own
week — week 2 and week 5 side by side under the same heading, which is
the difference that matters to a coach. Someone who already has a plan
keeps it and the coach is told so. Removing someone leaves their plan.
The old derived groups (same race, same date) stay underneath as
suggestions, each with "Make this a cycle". Members' plan headers name
the cycle.

A plan with no race counts down to its last day; cycles filter by distance

The home screen's countdown card used to say "— · no goal race set" for an
athlete on a plan of their own. It now counts to the plan's last day — "23
days · to the end of Autumn block" — with the same week-of-N progress bar.
The coach's Cycles screen gained one row of distance chips (All · 5K · 10K
· Half · Marathon, with counts): several cycles can share a distance now,
